## A Practical Review Routine
Start with the business objective and the product economics for the ASINs receiving most of the spend. Sort by spend before reviewing low-volume campaigns. The largest spend concentrations deserve attention first. Move from campaign totals into search terms, targets, placements and advertised ASINs before making a broad change. Check inventory, price, reviews, delivery and the live listing whenever relevant traffic stops converting. Write the action and the reason for it. Recheck after enough traffic has accumulated to judge the result.

## 30-Day Account Plan
Week 1: Baseline — Start with fix the product page before buying more traffic. Review main image and current price comparison. Save the current campaign, product and retail baseline before making changes. Record spend, sales, orders, CPC, conversion and ACoS for the campaigns controlling most of the account outcome. Keep inventory, price, reviews and delivery beside the ad data so the first review does not confuse a retail change with a PPC problem.
Week 2: Traffic and Targeting — Move into use discovery to find more demand. Work from the highest-spend campaigns downward and check Auto Broad Phrase and controlled discovery bids. Pull the reports needed for the decision instead of relying on the Campaign Manager total. Write the search terms, targets, placements, ASINs or audiences that deserve action and keep low-volume noise out of the change list.
Week 3: Controlled Changes — Apply changes around give proven keywords more control in controlled batches. Use separate high-spend terms and use placement reporting as the first checks. Keep the edit list short enough that you can tell what caused the next result. For larger accounts, keep an untouched source file and validate every bulk upload. Do not rebuild working campaigns merely to create a cleaner structure.
Week 4: Read the Result — Read add product targeting again after the account has collected enough traffic. Compare direct competitors and higher-priced competitors against the baseline from week one. Keep the changes that improved the intended business result, reverse changes that clearly damaged it, and separate inconclusive targets from proven winners or losers. The next month should start from what the account learned, not from the same generic checklist.
At the end of the month, write three decisions only: where more spend is justified, where spend needs to be reduced or redirected, and which performance problem belongs outside PPC. That keeps the account review connected to money, inventory and the product offer.
